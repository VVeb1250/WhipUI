Read for a heuristic review, not as a compulsory full audit for every UI edit.

Inspect the actual task through these lenses:
- Status: can the person tell whether an action is pending, completed or failed?
- Language: do labels match the audience's terms and expectations?
- Control: can they leave, undo, correct, or safely recover?
- Consistency: do equivalent actions behave alike and fit the platform?
- Prevention: can foreseeable mistakes be prevented without needless friction?
- Recognition: is needed context available where a decision is made?
- Efficiency: are repeat tasks efficient without obscuring first-use actions?
- Relevance: does the information hierarchy support the current goal?
- Recovery: do errors explain the problem and offer an actionable next step?
- Help: is task-specific assistance available when needed?

For each real issue, record task, visible evidence, consequence and correction.
Prioritize blocked tasks or potential loss first, then serious confusion or
recovery failures, then friction and polish. Frequency must be observed or marked
unknown. Avoid making a numeric score look like measured user success.
A heuristic is a lens, not proof that an unfamiliar pattern is wrong.
